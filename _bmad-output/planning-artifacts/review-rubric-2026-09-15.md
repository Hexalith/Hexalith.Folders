# PRD Quality Review — Hexalith.Folders

*Reviewed 2026-09-15 against `.claude/skills/bmad-prd/assets/prd-validation-checklist.md`. Subject: `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prd.md` (status `final`, `lastEdited: 2026-09-15`), immediately after its reconciliation with the sponsor-approved `sprint-change-proposal-2026-09-15.md`. No `addendum.md` exists.*

## Overall verdict

This remains a strong product contract. Its thesis is stated and re-stated without drift, its Non-Goals and Assumptions machinery does real work, and its evidence discipline is unusually honest and mechanically checkable — the proposal digest in the PD table verifies byte-for-byte, and all nineteen cited governance and contract artifacts plus `FolderStateTransitions.cs` exist at the paths given. What is at risk is the decision-tracking layer that this revision actually touched. The PD8 resolution ("cleartext confidential values are never made durable") is contradicted by three passages the revision left standing, one of which still calls the question an open architecture decision; the approval-state tables were not reopened by the decisions that reopen them (OQ3 still reads "Closed", C3 still reads Legal-approved); the Epic 12/13 admission never reached the Product Scope or MVP Feature Set sections, so the scope spine now disagrees with the posture section; and OQ11–OQ13 and NFR74–NFR84 are the least bounded content in a document that elsewhere pins every number to a C-criterion.

The PRD is usable today for direction and for release-gate construction. It is not yet safe to source-extract the confidentiality rule, the MVP scope list, or the new release bars without reconciling them first.

## Decision-readiness — adequate

The revision gets the hardest part right. It refuses to launder a sponsor's `continue` into role approval: the Current Delivery Posture, the PD table preamble, every one of the seven resolved rows, and `.memlog.md` all say "sponsor-approved with attestation pending", name the seven outstanding roles, and state that the general execution freeze stays in force. Each resolved row carries approvers, timestamp, proposal path, and the SHA-256, and retains the superseded question verbatim rather than deleting the history. `Admission records the requirement; it is not evidence that the requirement is met` appears as a standing note on both new NFR blocks. Trade-offs are named with what was given up — the incident-view paragraph in Product Scope names the rejected alternative ("leaving the console unavailable whenever projections degrade") and the accepted cost, and PD2's default (b) states plainly that a non-resumable task "leaves that repository/ref serialized for the tenant until a post-MVP repair operation exists". The Open Release Items are genuinely open: owners, blocking consequence, revisit condition, accountable approvers, and a closing rule that says "Delivery completion or a passing test alone cannot close an item."

The failure is one level down. Resolving seven PD items changes the status of things the PRD records elsewhere, and those records were not touched. A decision-maker reading the C9 row, the Security NFR bullet, or the Observability NFR bullet would come away with the superseded model and, in the C9 case, would believe PD8 is still undecided. A decision-maker reading the Open Release Items table would believe OQ1–OQ4 are settled, when PD10's own row says OQ3 reapproval is "pending". The document's two authoritative status surfaces — the Deferred Quantitative Targets table and the Open Release Items table — now lag the PM Decisions Index by one revision.

## Substance over theater — strong

Very little here is furniture. The NFRs carry product-specific thresholds rather than adjectives — 100 requested paths, 2,000 tree entries, 262,144-byte bounded range, 1,048,576-byte aggregate, 2-second execution limit, 30/15/60-second C7 profile, seven named C3 retention classes — and each is bound to a C-criterion with a governance record and an evidence path. The five personas are load-bearing: each maps to a row in the Actors table with an authority source and an FR list, UJ2 records a deliberate merge ("persona merged with UJ6/UJ9"), and Asha exists specifically to exercise the delegated-service-agent authority intersection rather than to pad the roster. The Vision section is anchored to SM7 rather than written as aspiration. Counter-metrics are real: CM5 ("an integration that reads context through Folders but writes files or commits through its own provider access does not count as adopted") closes the hollow-adoption loophole that SM7 would otherwise leave open.

The anti-theater instinct is the document's best quality. The provider-catalog paragraph records three gaps and then refuses to let them dissolve the requirement: "`PG2` is the recorded non-conformance against the bounded retry/backoff requirement above: the requirement stands unmet rather than being withdrawn." The Current Delivery Posture ends with an explicit list of evidence kinds that do not count — "Safe-empty, seed-only, unavailable, no-op, fake-backed, numerically mapped, structural, or documentation-only evidence may prove safety or contract shape but does not prove positive runtime capability." Very few PRDs at this stage write that sentence about themselves.

## Strategic coherence — adequate

The thesis holds and does work. "Turning project storage into a task lifecycle API" is stated in What Makes This Special, restated as one job in the MVP Contract Summary, and enforced in scope: the Product Brief's local-storage MVP was explicitly narrowed, and the PRD says so out loud ("This is an intentional scope change from the Product Brief"). Feature prioritization follows the thesis rather than ease — the hardest things (tenant isolation, idempotency across every mutation, failure visibility) are the explicitly uncuttable ones, stated twice: "Security, idempotency, failure visibility, and cross-surface semantics are not optional cuts." The success metrics validate the thesis instead of measuring activity; SM8 measures whether an agent can find its edit target within C4 limits, which is the actual bet.

The coherence problem is new and localized. Admitting Epic 12 as "an MVP foundation" is the largest scope statement this revision makes, and it reached only five places: the frontmatter edit history, the Current Delivery Posture, the Open Release Items, the PD table, and the two new NFR blocks. Product Scope → MVP - Minimum Viable Product, MVP Feature Set → Must-Have Capabilities, and MVP Acceptance Evidence were not touched. A reader extracting "what is in the MVP" from the sections named for that purpose still gets a fourteen-bullet capability list with no durability, restart-safety, or multi-replica line, and an eight-bullet acceptance list whose parity bar ("The full per-cell C13 conformance suite plus SM1") makes no reference to surviving a restart. The proposal only mandated the Current Delivery Posture edit, so the letter was met — but the result is a document whose posture section and scope sections now describe different MVPs.

### Findings

These are the lists that epics, sprint planning, and story creation read for MVP membership. *Fix:* add one Must-Have bullet naming the durable data plane and restart-safe multi-replica operation as MVP foundations with a pointer to OQ11, and one MVP Acceptance Evidence bullet requiring the OQ11 vertical-slice evidence; reference Epic 13 hardening in the same way against OQ12/OQ13.
- **medium** Evidence ownership for NFR79–NFR83 is double-assigned and partly unassigned (§ MVP Contract Summary → Current Delivery Posture; § Open Release Items, OQ11–OQ13 rows) — the posture section says "Epic 13 is the release-hardening epic that owns the NFR74-NFR84 evidence", yet NFR79 ("must survive process restart") and NFR80 ("multi-replica deployments must converge on one authoritative state") are precisely what OQ11 asks, and OQ11 is Epic 12's release question. Meanwhile OQ12 claims only "the NFR74–NFR78 and NFR84 edge-security requirements" and OQ13 names no NFR at all, so NFR79–NFR83 have no OQ gate anywhere in the table. *Fix:* split the ownership sentence — Epic 12/OQ11 owns NFR79–NFR81, Epic 13/OQ12 owns NFR74–NFR78 and NFR84, Epic 13/OQ13 owns the envelope — and add the owning NFR range to each of the three OQ rows' blocking-consequence cells.

## Done-ness clarity — thin

For FR1–FR58 this dimension is strong, and it is why the PRD has survived this long. Almost every FR carries a verifiable consequence rather than an adjective: FR9 requires denial "before exposing folder, repository, credential, lock, file, audit, provider, or context information" and pins the test mechanism ("isolation tests assert zero protected-resource reads before the denial through the test-harness access counters"); FR34 embeds the six C4 numbers inline; FR41/FR42 state expiry behaviour and the exact code `idempotency_key_expired`. The Task and Lock Completion Model is a twelve-row outcome table with lifecycle, lock state, and permitted next action per row, plus a hard budget ("no more than five read-only evidence checks, and ends within 15 minutes"). Subjective qualifiers were deliberately hunted out in the 2026-09-08 pass and the document is nearly free of them.

The material this revision added does not meet that bar, and it is the material the release now hangs on. OQ11–OQ13 are phrased as yes/no questions with no thresholds; two of the three define their own success in terms of an undefined noun. The eleven new NFRs are the only NFRs in the document anchored to no C-criterion, no approved number, and no existing artifact — their sole evidence paths are three files marked "(to be created)". An engineer asked to close OQ13 today cannot determine what envelope to measure against, and an engineer asked to satisfy NFR82 cannot determine which metrics are "release-significant". This is survivable because the items are release gates rather than build instructions, but it inverts the document's own standard at exactly the point where the freeze is supposed to lift.

### Findings

- **high** OQ13 has no threshold and no cross-reference (§ Open Release Items, OQ13 row) — "Can the supported deployment profile meet **the approved performance and capacity envelope**?" and "Close when the named profile meets the approved envelope with same-commit evidence." The PRD already carries an approved envelope — C1 and C5 (4 tenants, 2 folders/tenant, 2 workspaces/tenant, 2 tasks/tenant, ≥1 op/sec), C4's six input bounds, and SM4's 1s/500ms/2s/500ms latency targets — but OQ13 cites none of them, so it is unclear whether OQ13 re-tests the approved envelope or defines a new one. Compare OQ1, which names its four numbers in the row itself. *Fix:* replace "the approved performance and capacity envelope" with an explicit reference to C1/C4/C5 and SM4/SM5, and state whether OQ13 is a deployment-profile re-validation of those approved numbers or a new envelope requiring its own approval.
- **high** OQ11's bar is unquantified and weaker than the Technical Success and SM1 bars it is supposed to gate (§ Open Release Items, OQ11 row; cross-check § Success Criteria → Technical Success and SM1) — OQ11 asks for "one restart-safe, **multi-replica**, end-to-end mutation and query vertical slice using the durable data plane and **a real provider path**". No replica count is given, and "a real provider path" is singular and unnamed, while Technical Success requires the workflow be proven "for **both** supported providers and **all four** contract surfaces" and SM1 requires "100% of declared release-calibration scenarios pass for GitHub and Forgejo through REST, SDK, CLI, and MCP". As written, an OQ11 closure on one provider through one surface would satisfy the MVP-completion gate while failing the MVP's own success criterion. *Fix:* state the replica count, name the provider(s) and surface(s) the slice must traverse, and state explicitly whether OQ11 is a narrower foundation gate that SM1 still supersedes.
- **medium** Three of the eleven new NFRs have no bound, artifact, or inventory (§ Non-Functional Requirements → Edge Security and Deployment Hardening; → Durable Operation and Release Evidence) — NFR78 requires that untrusted content not control commands, paths, templates, or rendered active content "without **validation or neutralization**" with no policy artifact named, where the comparable file-policy rule cites `docs/contract/file-context-contract-groups.md`; NFR80 requires multi-replica convergence "on one authoritative state" with no convergence bound, where the comparable freshness rule cites C2's 500 ms; NFR82 requires that "**every release-significant** metric and alert" have demonstrated emission, an owner, and fault-path evidence, with no inventory of release-significant metrics and no "(to be created)" placeholder for one. *Fix:* give each a named artifact or bound — a neutralization policy reference for NFR78, a convergence bound for NFR80, and a release-significant metric/alert inventory (marked to-be-created under OQ12) for NFR82.
- **medium** The new NFR categories are not wired into Verification Expectations, and NFR83 introduces a competing classification vocabulary (§ Non-Functional Requirements → Verification Expectations; → Durable Operation and Release Evidence) — Verification Expectations enumerates which categories need automated tests ("Security, tenant isolation, idempotency, provider contract, read-model determinism, and cross-surface contract compatibility") and which need release validation evidence ("Performance, accessibility, retention, backup/recovery, and operations-console usability"); neither list was extended to the two new categories. Separately, NFR83 mandates a four-way classification — "automated, operational, approval-bound, or reference-pending" — that does not match Verification Expectations' two-way split, with no statement of how they relate. *Fix:* add both new categories to the appropriate Verification Expectations list and state that NFR83's four-way classification is the release-evidence taxonomy that refines that split.

## Scope honesty — strong

Omissions are explicit and abundant. The Explicit MVP Non-Goals section carries eighteen bullets, several of which pre-empt exactly the assumptions a reader would otherwise make silently — "No first-class move/rename command", "No archived-folder restore, hard deletion, remote-repository deletion, or provider-history rewrite", "No incoming provider webhook ingestion". The Assumptions index has twenty-three rows with a named confirmer and a revisit date each, and the two withdrawn rows are retained as withdrawn rather than deleted ("A3 | Withdrawn 2026-09-08: the 24-hour mutation replay tier is recorded in the approved OQ8 design"), which preserves the audit trail of what was once inferred. Seven evidence paths are marked "(to be created; OQn)" so a reader cannot mistake a citation for an artifact. De-scoping is proposed openly: PD5 keeps body-content indexing out of MVP and says a future capability "needs a new stable requirement and story after C9 approval"; PD9 keeps FR58's move-to-Phase-2 option on the table with its consequence stated.

Open-items density is high in absolute terms — nine open OQs, four open PDs, seven attestation-pending PDs, twenty-one live assumptions — which the rubric would normally call a blocker on a green-light PRD. It is not one here, because this PRD does not claim green light: frontmatter carries `implementationReadiness: not-ready`, the posture section says release is blocked, and the freeze is stated as in force. The density is proportionate to a document that says so.

- **high** The PRD delegates scheduling authority to a manifest field that does not exist (§ MVP Contract Summary → Current Delivery Posture) — "the scheduling authority is the `execution_rank` wave model in `planning-story-manifest.yaml`, under which Epics 4, 6, and 10 depend on Epic 12 foundations and no item may depend on an equal or later rank." `planning-story-manifest.yaml` contains zero occurrences of `execution_rank` or `execution_waves` and is still `generated_on: '2026-08-04'`; proposal §5.5 requires its regeneration and `.memlog.md` records that "Sections 5.5 (manifest regeneration) … are likewise untouched by this PRD run". ## Downstream usability — adequate

Traceability is largely excellent and, unusually, verifiable. FR1–FR58 are contiguous and unique; UJ1–UJ9, SM1–SM8, CM1–CM5, C0–C13, A1–A23, PD1–PD11 and OQ1–OQ13 are all continuous with no gaps or duplicates. Every inline `[ASSUMPTION An]` tag resolves to an index row and every index row appears inline, except the two explicitly withdrawn ones. Each UJ has a named protagonist carrying context inline, and each closes with a "This journey reveals requirements for …" bridge that downstream story creation can extract directly. The Glossary is twenty-three rows and is actively enforced by FR1 ("documentation/schema checks fail on conflicting synonyms or state casing"), and the Actors table adds a synonym column that maps journey wording to canonical authorization names — a mechanism most PRDs lack. Cross-references resolve: all nineteen cited `docs/exit-criteria/*` and `docs/contract/*` artifacts exist, `FolderStateTransitions.cs` exists at `src/Hexalith.Folders/Aggregates/Folder/FolderStateTransitions.cs`, and the PD table's proposal SHA-256 `5d12ae4d…04104` verifies against the file.

The gap is that the new material introduced cross-references the PRD cannot resolve against itself. NFR IDs appear nowhere in the Non-Functional Requirements section — the bullets are unnumbered, and numbering lives only in `epics.md` and `docs/exit-criteria/nfr-traceability.md`. That was tolerable while NFR IDs appeared only in passing (the C7 and C12 rows cite NFR7/NFR21/NFR49). It is no longer tolerable now that OQ12's blocking scope is defined as "the NFR74–NFR78 and NFR84 edge-security requirements": a reader of this document cannot determine which bullets those are, and NFR84 is not even in the Edge Security section — it is the sixth bullet of Durable Operation and Release Evidence.

## Shape fit — strong

The shape is right. This is a chain-top, brownfield, API/backend living contract feeding architecture, epics, a planning manifest, and generated contract surfaces, and it is written that way: the load-bearing sections are Endpoint Specifications, Command and Query Contract, Task and Lock Completion Model, Error Codes, Workspace State and Concurrency, and the Actors/operation-family tables — capability-spec shape, correctly weighted. The authority hierarchy is stated explicitly rather than assumed ("This PRD prevails for product intent, actors, scope, safety invariants, and user-visible outcomes; the Contract Spine prevails for operation names, wire schemas, and closed error fields"), with the product-owned exceptions enumerated verbatim. Nine UJs would be over-formalization for a single-operator tool, but this is multi-stakeholder B2B with seven canonical actors and genuinely distinct authority boundaries, and each UJ exercises a different one; they earn their place. Brownfield references are accurate — every cited artifact exists, and existing versus new material is distinguished by dated revision notes on UJ2, FR56, and FR58.

The one drift is directional: the PRD is beginning to absorb delivery-tracking content that belongs to the manifest and sprint tracker.

### Findings

- **low** The PRD now carries story-level lifecycle tracking (§ Assumptions and PM Decisions Index → PM decisions resolved, PD4 and PD5) — PD4's approved decision enumerates per-story statuses ("Stories 3.10, 3.12, 10.7, 11.2, and 11.3 stay `done`; Story 10.8 is corrected from `done` to `in-progress`… Story 11.4 stays `review`") and PD5 records a story retitle.
