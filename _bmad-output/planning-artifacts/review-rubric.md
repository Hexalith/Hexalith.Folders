## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Most of this dimension is now solid. The context-query scoping rule is one sentence with no hole ("A workspace whose lifecycle is `ready`, `locked`, or `committed` is readable by any actor holding the folder `read` grant … a workspace in `changes_staged`, `dirty`, `unknown_provider_outcome`, or `reconciliation_required` is readable only by the lock-owning task"). Three conflicts survive inside the state model itself, and they are the kind that get resolved silently by whoever writes the aggregate first. (1) §Workspace State and Concurrency defines "`dirty` means mutations are present and the lock lapsed, was revoked, or a commit failed, or reconciliation confirmed a partial set", but completion-table row 3 puts a lease lapse with zero mutations in `dirty` ("Lease lapses before any staged change | `dirty` | `expired`, then `stale` … The approved C6 matrix orphans the workspace rather than returning it to `ready`"). The row is accurate to the approved matrix (architecture.md C6: "`locked` → `dirty` | `LockLeaseExpired` (no mutations applied yet) | Lock orphaned"), so the defect is the PRD's definition; the consequences are that a benign no-op lapse counts against CM2's 5% recovery-burden budget, that FR30 makes such a workspace ineligible for cleanup forever ("Dirty … workspaces are not cleanup-eligible"), and that PD2's scope ("a task that can never resume while changes remain staged or the workspace is `reconciliation_required`") excludes the case where nothing is staged at all, so a crashed agent that never mutated wedges the repository/ref with nothing to lose. (2) A18 (completion-table row 6) says a workspace made `inaccessible` by revocation "returns to `dirty` with its changes intact" when authority is restored, while the Glossary makes `inaccessible` task-terminal ("The current task has ended in `committed`, `failed`, `inaccessible`, or explicit no-change closure … then provides the same C3 seven-day observation window before cleanup") and FR30's exclusion list omits `inaccessible`, so on day eight the working files are gone and A18 is false. (3) The Actors table gives the tenant member (Nadia) FR11 and FR18 and UJ1 has her run readiness, create the repository-backed folder, and prepare the workspace, but the family table grants "Readiness and provider evidence" to "Tenant administrator authority or operator permission" only and puts "create a repository-backed folder or bind a pre-created repository" under folder `administer`; nothing says who holds `administer` on a freshly created logical folder, and with "administer implies neither `write` nor `read`" plus "absence of an applicable allow denies", a tenant administrator who has not been granted `administer` on the folder cannot execute FR5 or FR13 on it either. The Spine does declare `tenant-context-and-folder-create-permission` on `CreateFolder`, so the tenant-level half is real; the bootstrap of the folder-level half is simply unstated.

One cardinality question is also open: row 2 says "Task closes without a commit; later work requires a new task", but the PRD bounds only "one task identity binds to at most one workspace" and never says whether a second task may lock a `ready` workspace another task prepared or must prepare its own.

### Findings
- **[high]** `dirty` is defined as "mutations are present" but the completion model and the approved C6 matrix assign `dirty` to a zero-mutation lease lapse (§Workspace State and Concurrency "`dirty` means mutations are present and the lock lapsed…" vs. Task and Lock Completion Model row 3 "Lease lapses before any staged change | `dirty`"; architecture.md C6 "`locked` → `dirty` | `LockLeaseExpired` (no mutations applied yet)"; FR30 "Dirty … workspaces are not cleanup-eligible"; PD2 scope "while changes remain staged or the workspace is `reconciliation_required`") — An engineer implementing the definition and a tester working from the table disagree on the first edge case; a crashed agent that never mutated leaves a repository/ref serialized for the tenant with nothing staged, PD2 does not cover it, and CM2 counts it as recovery burden. *Fix:* Redefine `dirty` as "the lock lapsed, was revoked, a commit failed, or reconciliation confirmed a partial set, whether or not mutations are present; a `dirty` workspace with an empty staged set is returned to `ready` by the owning task's release" and either (a) extend PD2 to the empty-staged-set case or (b) add a product rule that a `dirty` workspace with no staged changes becomes cleanup-eligible and returns to `ready`/`unlocked` at the C7 `stale` threshold, and exclude that case from CM2.
- **[high]** A18's "changes intact" promise is contradicted by `inaccessible` being task-terminal and cleanup-eligible (Task and Lock Completion Model row 6 / A18 "the workspace returns to `dirty` with its changes intact"; Glossary "Task-terminal: The current task has ended in `committed`, `failed`, `inaccessible`… then provides the same C3 seven-day observation window before cleanup"; FR30 "deletes temporary working files at the C3 seven-day boundary. Dirty, unknown-provider-outcome, and reconciliation-required workspaces are not cleanup-eligible") — After the seven-day window the working files are deleted while the lifecycle stays `inaccessible`, so a restoration on day eight yields `dirty` with no changes, or (per A10) content-unavailable; the cleanup story and the revocation story will encode opposite rules. *Fix:* State one of: "an `inaccessible` workspace with a non-empty staged set is not task-terminal and not cleanup-eligible while the revoking authority can be restored" (then amend the Glossary and FR30), or "A18 applies only within the seven-day observation window; after cleanup, restoration yields `dirty` with content-unavailable and no recoverable changes" (then amend A18).
- **[high]** The actor × family table cannot run UJ1 and has no bootstrap for folder-level `administer` (§Protected operation families "Readiness and provider evidence | Tenant | … | Tenant administrator authority or operator permission" vs. UJ1 "She starts with a provider readiness check" and Actors table "Tenant member … FR11, FR18"; "Folder administration | … create a repository-backed folder or bind a pre-created repository … | `administer`" with no rule for the creator's initial grant; "`administer` implies neither `write` nor `read`" and "absence of an applicable allow denies" vs. FR5/FR13 "Tenant administrators can grant and revoke … can archive") — A tenant member has no path to readiness or to `CreateRepositoryBackedFolder` on a folder she just created, and a tenant administrator without an explicit `administer` grant has no path to FR5/FR13 on any folder. *Fix:* Add two product rules under the family table: "`CreateFolder` grants the creating principal `administer` on the new folder (recorded as the first ACL entry and audited)" and "tenant-administrator authority implies `administer` on every folder in the tenant (not `write`/`read`, per A12/A17)"; and add "tenant member with the folder-create permission" to the readiness family's grant column, or state in UJ1 that readiness is re-evaluated inside `CreateRepositoryBackedFolder` and drop the explicit check.
- **[medium]** Workspace-to-task cardinality after release is undefined (Task and Lock Completion Model row 2 "Task closes without a commit; later work requires a new task"; Task identity "one task identity binds to at most one workspace"; Glossary "Workspace: Disposable task working area prepared from a repository binding") — The PRD bounds one direction only; whether `LockWorkspace` by a second task on a `ready` workspace prepared by the first is a lock-conflict, a safe denial, or allowed decides the shape of the lock aggregate and the meaning of "prepared by the caller's task". *Fix:* Add one sentence: "A workspace belongs to the task that prepared it for its lifetime; a different task must prepare its own workspace (which the serializing identity then gates), and `LockWorkspace` by any other task on it is denied with the lock-conflict result even when `unlocked`."
- **[low]** Open qualifiers survive outside the NFR lock (§Architectural Boundaries "cleanup/expiration support where needed"; §SDK Requirements "async operations where applicable"; FR57 "where it affects operational readiness"; FR2 "documents and demonstrates" with no definition of a demonstration, deferred by PD7) — Each leaves the reader to guess the rule; the throttling list, by contrast, is now closed with "exactly". *Fix:* Replace "where needed/applicable" with the condition ("cleanup/expiration support for every provider port that leaves temporary working files"; "async for every operation the Spine marks accepted"), bound FR57 to the FR23 evidence fields, and define FR2's demonstration as "a runnable example per surface in the SDK quickstart and CLI/MCP reference that exercises the ordered lifecycle including at least one failure transition".
- **[low]** Operator disposition labels every healthy active task "degraded" (§Workspace State and Concurrency "locked/changes-staged are degraded-but-serving"; the approved C6 catalog says the same) — A console that derives disposition from this rule warns on normal in-progress work. *Fix:* Rename to "serving-with-active-task" in both the PRD and C6 (one lockstep change, since C6 agrees today).
- **[low]** PD6 does not list every NFR bullet that still fails this dimension (PD6 scope vs. §Scalability "must remain queryable as folder history grows", "without making routine status, audit, or context queries unusable"; §Observability "within a defined status-freshness target" (C2 is defined; cite it), "sanitized error category where applicable"; §Performance "retry hints where available") — Bullets not named in PD6 will survive the relock unchanged. *Fix:* Add these five to PD6's list.
